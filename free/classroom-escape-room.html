How it works without a single lock

The premise: the library’s rarest book is sealed in the Archive Cabinet and the five-digit code was split across five stations. Each station holds exactly one digit, so no group can open it alone — the class has to pool the code at the end.

The “lock” is simply you. A group solves a station, shows you their answer, and only a correct one earns them that station’s digit off your answer strip. A wrong one gets “not yet” and they keep working. That is the entire mechanism, which is why there is nothing to buy, nothing to laminate and nothing to re-lock between classes.

Common questions

Do I need padlocks or a lockbox?

No. There is no hardware anywhere in this activity. The “Archive” is any box, drawer, cupboard or large envelope you seal before the lesson.

Do I have to give an email address?

No. The download link above is the PDF file itself. There is no form, no list and no purchase.

Will it work with 30 students?

Yes — it is built for five groups of five or six, with all five stations running at once so nobody waits.

How long is the reset between periods?

About five minutes. Only the Code Trackers get used up, so you collect the signs, keep your answer strips, re-seal the box and hand out fresh trackers.

Can I use my own review questions?

Yes, and the pack tells you exactly how. Each station’s answer-key strip carries a swap note, and because the digit sits on your strip rather than in the puzzle, changing the questions never changes the code.
